# DOT physicals and certification exams

Only Certified Medical Examiners (CME) listed on the FMCSA National Registry can perform DOT physicals. It isn’t a courtesy you can extend to a patient with a commercial driver's license. Certification requires training and an exam.

CMEs often do their work out of urgent care offices, and typically don’t have the patient’s medical records. They have the patients fill out forms about their medical issues. When appropriate, a CME may contact you as the PCP for clarification about certain medical facts, such as a diabetic’s last a1c or are they on insulin; why they are on medications for ADHD. Of course, you can only reply with the patient’s written consent on our own “Authorization For Release of Health Information”. **If you are not a CME, you supply data; you do not make any decisions.**

You can [go through the process to become a CME](https://nationalregistry.fmcsa.dot.gov/register). But be very wary of doing DOT physicals on your own patients. Certifying your own patients puts you in two roles with different clients: as their physician you serve them, but as a CME you serve the federal government and the other drivers, and the pull to give a long-standing patient the benefit of the doubt is strong. Here's an example that would put you in an awkward position: if a truck driver's systolic blood pressure is over 140 mmHg at the time of the exam with you, that negatively impacts their renewal. You might know that *typically* their pressure is under 140 ... but by federal regulations that does not count.

Doing DOT physicals for your patients can also negatively impact your therapeutic relationship, because once the patient knows you control his commercial driver's license, he has a reason to stop telling you about the daytime sleepiness or the drinking.
