# Time off for work-related stress

This is not as unreasonable as it may sound, and is a common use for FMLA. But it’s not a vacation. The time has to be spent seeing a therapist or psychiatrist regularly, and starting appropriate treatment. You can’t guarantee your patient will be paid, and you can’t guarantee their employer will be persuaded just to agree. Give them a note to get them through their first psychology appointment, and forward any further paperwork from the employer to their mental health professional. (I’ve been burned when it turned out that an employer required the mental health professional to complete the paperwork — but only found out long after the fact.)

It is *not* your job to investigate workplace conflicts, assign blame, or solve their problem. Your job is to:

- obtain a complete psychiatric history, which is often missing in a patient's EHR (there's no special section for it)
- list their current work-related impairments (eg, inabililty to focus, interact professionally with the public)
- list their current impairments *outside* of work (you will be asked this specifically on paperwork)
- start what medical therapy you may deem appropriate
- arrange a referral to an appropriate behavioral health professional, or note that they are already seeing one; and
- ask if accommodations of some sort would help (eg part-time work for a while)

You should also point out that while you will do what you can, you, nor does any health care provider, have the ability to change someone's work environment. There's no pill for nasty coworkers or impossible expectations. Sometimes, the best therapy is leaving that particular job. I will broach that sometimes by asking if they have considered it, or is it even an option — but never suggest it.

Two things to hold in mind at once. This category does get abused; an HR friend of mine says it’s common for people at his company on the verge of being fired to use it as a ploy for a few more weeks of salary. But psychiatric conditions are also FMLA-qualifying and ADA-covered on exactly the same terms as physical ones, and the person in front of you may be genuinely ill.

Mental health days are a real things, and time off work can help your patient keep their job, and help their employer keep their employee.
